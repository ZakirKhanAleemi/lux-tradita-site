The tradition behind it

Millennia before the zodiac, Egyptian astronomers divided the sky into 36 decans — stars whose risings marked the hours of the night and the weeks of the year. Their calendar ran three seasons of four months, plus five epagomenal days, the birthdays of the gods. In the Amduat, the sun's nightly passage through the twelve hours of the Duat became a map of renewal: descent, trial, and return at dawn.

The app computes the Egyptian date and the presiding decan from real astronomy, on your device, and shapes each day's practice around it.

Common questions

What is the Duat? In Egyptian cosmology, the Duat is the realm the sun crosses each night — twelve hours of darkness before rebirth at dawn. The app borrows it as a frame for daily renewal: each day closes, is examined, and begins again.
Why five "extra" days? The civil year was 360 days — twelve months of thirty. The five epagomenal days added at the end belonged to no month; tradition made them the birthdays of Osiris, Horus, Set, Isis, and Nephthys. The app marks them.
Does it work offline? The calendar and decans are computed on the device. The daily scenario and Ask need a connection to our generation service.
