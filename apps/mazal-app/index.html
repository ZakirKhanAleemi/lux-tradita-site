Mazal

"Mazal" is your constellation — a daily practice in Kabbalistic astrology: the mazalot, the Tree of Life, and the work of tikkun.

How it works

The same short walkthrough shown the first time you open the app:

  • ✡ Mazal — Kabbalistic astrology in the Hebrew calendar's own rhythm — each month a mazal, each day a sephirah, computed from the real calendar. The tendency is given; how you live it is yours.
  • Twelve Months, Twelve Mazalot — Each Hebrew month carries one of the twelve mazalot — the constellations the Sefer Yetzirah ties to the twelve simple letters. This month's mazal sets the quality of the daily practice.
  • Your Tikkun — The month you were born under names not just a character but a correction — a tikkun, the quality your soul is here to refine. Each day's practice weaves this month's sky with your own.
  • Ask Anything — Ask about a month, a mazal, a sephirah, or the tradition itself — the current month and your birth details are already in view. Plain language, any time.
  • Walk the Path — cheshbon ha-nefesh, in a modern form. Read the month's mazal and today's sephirah, do each day's short practice, and watch your five-quality star grow — Loving-Kindness, Strength, Harmony, Victory, Gratitude.
  • Add Your Birth Date — Your birth date finds your Hebrew birth month — your natal mazal and its tikkun — and colours every daily practice with it. Optional, and everything stays on your device.

Getting started

  1. Open the Today tab — see today's mazal (constellation influence) and the quality it invites you to work with.
  2. Take the daily practice — a short, ordinary scenario shaped by the day. Respond as you naturally would.
  3. Enter your birth date in Settings to personalise the practice with your own mazal.
  4. Explore the Tree and Tikkun tabs — the map of the sefirot, and the repair-work the tradition ties to your sign.

The tradition behind it

When Jewish tradition says mazal tov, it literally wishes you "a good constellation." The mazalot are the twelve constellations as the rabbis and medieval Kabbalists read them — influences to work with, never fates to submit to: ein mazal l'Yisrael, the tradition insists — the constellation inclines, it does not compel.

The Tree of Life arranges ten sefirot — qualities like lovingkindness, severity, and balance — into a map of inner life. Tikkun is the daily repair: noticing where a quality runs to excess or falls short, and adjusting. The app turns that into a concrete daily practice.

Common questions

Is astrology even allowed in Jewish tradition? The tradition's own answer is nuanced: the Talmud debates it, and the dominant view — ein mazal l'Yisrael — is that the constellations incline but never bind. The app takes exactly that stance: reflection, not prediction.
Do I need any Kabbalah background? None. Every term is explained in plain language where it appears, and the daily practice never requires theory — it's a short scenario and an honest response.
Does it work offline? The calendar and Tree are on-device. The daily scenario and Ask need a connection to our generation service.
